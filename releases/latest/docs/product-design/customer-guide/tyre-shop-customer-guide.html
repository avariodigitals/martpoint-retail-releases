What a tyre business does every day

Your trade has three moving parts: tyres sold by size, fitting work done on customer vehicles, and supplier accounts that keep the shelves full. MartPoint runs all three as one tyre business — not a general shop with car parts bolted on.

Always do

  • Set the business type to Tyre Shop before stocking anything.
  • Add each tyre size as a size option under its brand — 195/65R15, 205/55R16, and so on.
  • Record the batch or DOT code when you receive stock, so old tyres can be found later.
  • Register a customer’s vehicle when they come in for fitting.
  • Pick the vehicle on the bill so fitting history stays with the car.

Never do

  • Create one product per size (“Gondwana 195”, “Gondwana 205”…) — sizes belong under one tyre.
  • Sell from the oldest stock last. Fit and sell the oldest DOT batch first.
  • Bill a fitting without selecting the customer’s vehicle.
  • Leave a vehicle unregistered — next time the customer comes, the history is gone.
  • Reorder only from memory. The Size Alerts card tells you what is actually low.

Before your first sale

  • Set the business type — choose Tyre Shop in Business Profile.
  • Stock brands by size — every brand carries its own size list.
  • Add fitting services — fitting, balancing, alignment, puncture repair.
  • Register vehicles — plate, make and model for each regular customer.

Service that keeps cars coming back

  • Remember the vehicle — open their fit history before they explain the problem.
  • Quote before fitting — bill lines show exactly what the customer agreed to.
  • Watch stock age — nobody should leave with a tyre that sat five years on the rack.
  • Plan for the rains — the seasonal trend shows when demand turns.

Tyre shop golden rule

A tyre is only fully described by its size and its batch. The size tells the customer it fits their car; the batch (DOT code) tells you how old the rubber is. MartPoint tracks both — one by size option, one by Batch/DOT code on the stock — so you never sell either blind.

Tell MartPoint you are a tyre shop

Bola runs Advance Tyres. Before anything else, she tells the system what the business is, so the whole workspace starts speaking tyre language.

Bola, Owner

“We sell tyres and fitting work, not general goods. I want the system to talk about sizes, DOT codes, depots and fitting services — and I want my staff to see the same thing I do.”

What Bola does in MartPoint

  1. She opens More → Business Profile on the phone, or Business Profile on the desktop.
  2. She picks Tyre Shop as the industry type and saves.
  3. Menus, buttons and reports switch to tyre wording on their own: items become Tyres, batches become Batch/DOT Code, expiry checks become Stock Age, services become Fitting Services, and storages become Depots.
  4. She keeps the extras a tyre shop needs — vehicle records, fitting services, warranty notes and staff commission — all switched on by the Tyre Shop profile.
Why the profile matters: the business type decides which tools appear and what everything is called. A tyre shop and a chemist run on the same system but never see the same screens or the same words.

Late morning

Stock tyres by brand and size

Advance Tyres carries Gondwana, Hallburg and GT Radial. Each brand comes in many sizes, and each size sells out on its own — so the catalogue must track stock per size, not per brand.

Bola, Owner

“Last month 195/65R15 flew out and 225/45R17 sat for six weeks. If my stock list only says ‘Gondwana — 40 pieces’, I cannot see that, and I reorder blind.”

What Bola does in MartPoint

  1. She opens Tyre List → Add and creates one record per tyre line: “Gondwana 5Star”.
  2. Under the tyre, she adds size options: 195/65R15, 205/55R16, 225/45R17… Each size keeps its own price and its own stock count.
  3. She sets a reorder (alert) level per size, so a low size raises its own alarm.
  4. She files tyres under categories that match how customers shop — Passenger Car, SUV & 4x4, Truck & Commercial, Rims & Wheels, Tubes/Flaps/Valves.
  5. Rims, tubes, flaps and valves go in as ordinary stock lines next to the tyres.

What one tyre record holds

Brand lineGondwana 5Star — one record, many sizes
Size options195/65R15, 205/55R16… each with stock and price

One record per brand line, one size option per size. Creating a separate product for every size splits your history and doubles your work. Sizes are options under one tyre — the way the trade actually counts stock.

Midday

Receive stock and keep the rubber honest

A distributor delivery arrives: twelve Gondwana 195/65R15 from one batch, eight Hallburg 185/65R15 from another. Tyres age even sitting on the shelf, so Bola records which batch each one came from.

Bola, Owner

“A five-year-old tyre is a return waiting to happen, no matter how good the tread looks. When stock comes in, I need to know which week of which year it was made — that is what the DOT code stamped on the sidewall tells me.”

What Bola does in MartPoint

  1. She raises a Purchase against the distributor account so the supplier balance and stock arrival stay in one story.
  2. On receiving, she records the stock into her Depot and enters the Batch/DOT Code on the batch field for each size.
  3. The system’s Stock Age Check watches the batch dates and surfaces ageing batches on the dashboard.
  4. When fitting, staff sell the oldest batch first — first-in, first-out — so nothing rots on the rack.

What the batch record gives you

Depots, not “warehouses”: in the Tyre Shop profile every storage location is called a Depot — the main shop rack, the back store, or another branch. Stock transfers move sizes between them with a full paper trail.

Know the car, not just the customer

Mr Okafor drives in with a 2015 Toyota Corolla, plate ABC-1234. He is a regular, but last time it was a different fitter who saw him. Bola wants the vehicle — not the memory of one staff member — to hold the history.

Bola, Owner

“When he says ‘the usual size’, I want the system to say what the usual size is. And when another car comes in for the same size, I want to know which vehicles on file need that size soon.”

What the counter staff do in MartPoint

  1. They open Customer Vehicles → Add Customer Vehicle (desktop) or Customer Vehicles on the phone.
  2. They record make, model, year, license plate and mileage, plus notes like “runs 205/55R16, prefers all-seasons”.
  3. The status chip shows where the car is: On file, In the workshop, or Back with owner.
  4. After any fitting billed to the vehicle, History opens the full trail: every visit, what went on it, what was paid.

What the register is — and what it is not

Plate first, always. The quickest way back into a customer’s story is their plate: “ABC-1234, two Hallburg fittings last March.” That is the difference between a tyre shop that remembers and one that starts over every visit.

Fitting bay

Sell, fit and bill without double entry

Okafor is changing two front tyres and needs balancing. Tunde the fitter works from the till screen while the car is still on the ramp.

Tunde, Tyre Fitter

“Two 195/65R15 off the oldest batch, balancing on both, fitted now. I do not want to write it on paper and have the counter key it in again after close.”

What Tunde does in MartPoint

  1. At the till he picks the customer, then the vehicle from their vehicles list — the picker shows plate and model.
  2. He adds the tyre sizes from search — typing a size like “195/65” or a brand name finds the line immediately.
  3. He adds the fitting services: Fitting, Wheel Balancing, whatever the job actually took.
  4. He takes payment and finalises. Stock drops per size, the old batch moves out first, and the job is recorded against the vehicle.
  5. The printed bill shows tyres and services as separate lines — the customer sees what they are paying for.

What one bill updates on its own

Commission & accountsFitter share and depot totals follow the bill
Bill to the vehicle. If the job is not linked to the car, the fitment history and the “what size does this customer run” answer both go blank. Choosing the vehicle takes two taps and protects the whole record.

Closing up

The dashboard that plans the next season

Before close, Bola opens the dashboard. The Tyre Shop Overview answers her three nightly questions: what is low, what is old, and what is the year telling her.

Bola, Owner

“In our market, demand turns with the rains — potholes and blown tyres follow the first storms, and everyone hunts for the same sizes at once. If I start ordering when the rush starts, I am two weeks late.”

What the Tyre Shop dashboard shows

  1. Sizes on File — how many sized tyres you carry, and how many sit below their reorder level.
  2. Size Alerts — the exact sizes to reorder first, lowest stock on top.
  3. Vehicles on File & Fitments — the workshop register and how many bills went to a vehicle in the last 30 days.
  4. Aged Stock & DOT Checks — batches ageing on the shelf, before a customer has to ask about them.
  5. Seasonal Tyre Sales — tyres sold and earned month by month for the last twelve months. Rising bars before the rains are your early warning: that is when customers start hunting, and when your order should already be placed.

The reorder habit that pays

WeeklyClear the Size Alerts list against the distributor account
MonthlyCheck the seasonal bars against last year’s same month
Before the rainsThicken the fast-selling sizes while the depot still has room
QuarterlyWork through aged batches — discount or refit them before they expire
Cash and accounts travel with the stock: the Tyre Shop profile keeps full accounts on — distributor balances, customer credits and pay-plan terms, and the cash ledger — so purchasing decisions and money decisions are read from the same place.

Online

Your storefront, sizes and all

Customers who never see the ramp can still order from it. Advance Tyres’ public store shows the catalogue with sizes and prices, and every order lands in the same stock and billing flow as a walk-in.

Customer (online)

“Do you have 205/55R16 in stock? I saw it on your page. Can you hold a pair till Saturday and fit them that afternoon?”

What the public store already does

  • Every tyre shows its available sizes and prices; orderable sizes only show live stock truth.
  • Orders arrive in the workspace alongside counter sales — one stock count, one ledger.
  • Fitting services and depot details follow your settings; what you turn off is simply not shown.
  • A product without a photo shows your brand plate — never a random stock image.

Quick fixes

  • A size will not sell: check that size’s own stock count — each size keeps its own.
  • Menus still say “Product”: confirm the business type saved as Tyre Shop, then reload.
  • No vehicle picker at the till: the customer must be picked first, and the vehicle registered under their name.
  • Fit history empty: the bill was finalised without selecting a vehicle — history follows the bill, not the walk-in.
  • Demo content looks wrong: switch the industry in Business Profile; the shop then seeds only tyre examples.
