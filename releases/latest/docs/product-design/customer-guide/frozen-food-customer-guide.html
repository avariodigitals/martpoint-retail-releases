What a frozen food shop does every day

Your job is to keep the freezers cold, know exactly how many kilos and packs you have, and sell fast without guessing. MartPoint tracks every carton, kilo and pack — and warns you when a freezer gets too warm.

Always do

  • Check and record freezer temperature every morning and evening.
  • Enter the batch and expiry date for every carton you receive.
  • Sell the oldest stock first — first to expire, first to go out.
  • Weigh items at the POS so stock reduces by the exact kilos sold.
  • Count remaining stock before ordering a new carton.

Never do

  • Sell fish or meat that thawed and was refrozen.
  • Ignore a high temperature warning on a freezer.
  • Mix a new carton with an old batch without recording it.
  • Sell an item past its expiry date.
  • Guess weights — always weigh, so your stock stays accurate.

Before your first carton

  • Set the business type — choose Frozen Foods Retailer in Business Profile.
  • Add your freezers — name each one and set its safe temperature range.
  • Know your units — decide what you sell by kilo, by pack, or by carton.
  • Have a scale ready — frozen food is sold by weight.

Food safety habits

  • Log temperature twice a day — it is your proof that food stayed safe.
  • Watch expiry dates — MartPoint flags products close to expiry.
  • Record thaw time — once thawed, some items must sell within hours.
  • Move stock fast — if a freezer fails, relocate items and note it.

Frozen food golden rule

The cold chain is your business. If the temperature log shows a freezer went warm, check the stock before selling anything from it. Customers trust you because your turkey and fish are always properly frozen — protect that trust every day.

Morning

Tell MartPoint you sell frozen food

Funmi runs a frozen food shop in Ibadan. She sells frozen turkey, croaker and Titus fish, chicken gizzard and goat meat. Before adding any product, she tells MartPoint what kind of business she runs.

Funmi, Owner

“I buy cartons of frozen turkey and fish, keep them in two chest freezers, and sell by the kilo. Gizzard and goat meat I sell in 1 kg packs. I need the app to track kilos and packs, warn me before stock expires, and show me if a freezer is getting warm.”

What Funmi does in MartPoint

  1. She opens More → Business Profile.
  2. She picks Frozen Foods Retailer as the industry type. MartPoint automatically turns on the right features.
  3. She confirms Frozen Food Cold Chain is on — this adds the freezers and temperature log.
  4. She keeps Batch Tracking and Expiry Tracking on — every carton gets a batch and a date.
  5. She keeps Multi-Unit Selling on — so one product can sell by carton, kilo or pack.
  6. She saves the profile. A new Butchery & Frozen section appears under Operations, and a Cold Chain tile appears under More on mobile.
Why the profile matters: Choosing Frozen Foods Retailer gives you freezer management, temperature logging, expiry alerts and sell-by-weight at the POS — without any extra setup. Items are labelled “Product / Pack” which matches how frozen food is sold.

Late morning

Register the freezers

Funmi has two chest freezers: one for poultry (turkey, chicken, gizzard) and one for fish and goat meat. She adds both to MartPoint so every temperature reading is recorded against the right freezer.

Funmi, Owner

“Freezer A holds the turkey and gizzard. Freezer B holds the croaker, Titus and goat meat. If Freezer B ever goes above -15°C, I need to know immediately — that fish must not thaw.”

What Funmi does in MartPoint

  1. She opens Operations → Butchery & Frozen → Freezers. On mobile: More → Cold Chain.
  2. She adds Freezer A — Poultry and sets the safe range: minimum -25°C, maximum -15°C.
  3. She adds Freezer B — Fish & Meat with the same range.
  4. She opens the Temperature Log, picks Freezer A, and enters the morning reading: -18°C. She does the same for Freezer B.
  5. She repeats the check every evening before closing.

What the freezer record controls

Freezer nameFreezer A — Poultry, Freezer B — Fish & Meat
Safe rangeMin and max temperature the freezer should hold
Temperature logDated readings — your cold-chain proof
WarningsReadings outside the safe range are flagged
Power cuts happen: If the light goes off for hours, check the freezer thermometer before opening shop. Log the reading honestly — a flagged reading tells you to inspect the stock, not just the freezer.

Midday

Add the frozen products

A supplier just delivered cartons of turkey, croaker fish, gizzard and goat meat. Funmi adds each product with its unit, batch, expiry and storage details before the cartons go into the freezers.

Funmi, Owner

“The turkey carton is 10 kg and I sell it at ₦4,500 per kilo. Gizzard is packed in 1 kg packs at ₦4,000. Goat meat is ₦6,000 a kilo. Restaurants sometimes buy a whole carton — I need a wholesale price for that too.”

What Funmi does in MartPoint

  1. She goes to More → Inventory → Add Product.
  2. She names the product Frozen Turkey and sets the unit to KG.
  3. She enters the opening stock (10 kg from the carton), the batch number, and the expiry date on the carton.
  4. In the Butchery & Frozen section of the form, she sets storage temperature (-25°C to -15°C), thaw time, use-within hours after thaw, and frozen shelf life in days.
  5. She enters the retail price ₦4,500/kg and a wholesale price for full cartons.
  6. She repeats for Croaker Fish, Titus Fish, Chicken Gizzard (1 kg pack) and Goat Meat.

What the product form captures

Product & unitFrozen Turkey, sold in KG
Stock & batchKilos in stock, batch number, expiry date
StorageMin/max temperature, frozen shelf life
Thaw rulesThaw time and use-within hours after thawing
PricingRetail per kilo, wholesale per carton
Multi-unitSame product can sell by carton, pack or kilo
Expiry is money: Enter the expiry date from every carton, every time. MartPoint uses it to warn you before stock goes bad — and the batch number tells you exactly which delivery a product came from.

Afternoon

Sell by kilo, pack or carton

Tunde is at the counter. A customer wants 2.5 kg of turkey and one pack of gizzard. Later, a restaurant buys a full carton of croaker at the wholesale price.

Tunde, Sales Attendant

“The scale says 2.5 kg for the turkey — that is ₦11,250. Plus one pack of gizzard at ₦4,000. Total ₦15,250. The customer is paying ₦10,000 cash and ₦5,250 by transfer.”

Screen by screen at the POS

  1. Tunde taps POS on the bottom menu.
  2. He searches Turkey, taps the product and enters the weighed quantity: 2.5 kg.
  3. He adds Chicken Gizzard — 1 pack at ₦4,000.
  4. He taps Split Pay and enters Cash: ₦10,000 and Transfer: ₦5,250, then types the transfer reference.
  5. He taps Save. The receipt prints and stock drops by exactly 2.5 kg of turkey and 1 pack of gizzard.
  6. For the restaurant, he sells a full carton of croaker and taps the Wholesale price button — no need to create a separate product.

What happens behind the counter

As soon as the sale is saved:

StockTurkey drops by 2.5 kg, gizzard by 1 pack
Cash₦10,000 recorded in the cash drawer
Transfer₦5,250 marked with the reference ID
BatchThe sale is tied to the batch it came from
Retail vs wholesale: The POS has a retail price button and a wholesale price button. Walk-in customers get the per-kilo price; bulk buyers get the carton price. Both reduce the same stock correctly.

Evening

Online orders and closing checks

Funmi’s products are also on her online store, so customers order frozen packs for pickup or delivery. Before closing, she runs through a short checklist.

Customer (via WhatsApp)

“I saw the 1 kg gizzard pack on your store. Please keep two packs and 3 kg of goat meat for me — I will pick up tomorrow morning.”

Selling online

  1. Funmi publishes her products to the Online Store from the product list — turkey, fish, gizzard and goat meat appear with prices and photos.
  2. A customer places an order online or messages on WhatsApp after seeing the store link.
  3. Funmi confirms stock from the product list, sets the items aside in the freezer, and records the sale at pickup.
  4. Because batches are tracked, she packs from the oldest batch first so nothing expires at the back of the freezer.

The closing checklist

Every evening before locking up:

Temperature logRecord the evening reading for both freezers
Expiry checkReview products nearing expiry — push them first tomorrow
Low stockSee which products need a new carton order
Daily summaryReports → Daily Summary shows sales, cash and transfer totals
