Start here

Choose your factory mode

Nylon factories come in three flavours. MartPoint’s Nylon & Polythene profile supports all three — and a factory can run more than one at once (extrude for some orders, buy film for others, sell surplus rolls to neighbouring converters).

Mode A — Full line
Extrude film and convert it to bags

You buy resin, masterbatch and ink; the extruder blows film into rolls; the printer brands them; the cutting/sealing machine turns them into vest bags, flat bags or rolls of film. Full stage chain: allocation → extrusion → printing → cutting → packing → QC.

Mode B — Conversion only
Buy film rolls, convert them to bags

No extruder. You purchase finished film rolls from another factory and run them through printing and cutting/sealing. Purchased rolls enter stock at the roll stage and get consumed downstream — never double-deducted.

Mode C — Trading
Sell film rolls without converting

You buy (or produce surplus) film rolls and sell them as goods — by the roll or by the kg. No job, no stages: a normal sale through POS or an invoice, straight out of roll stock.

The whole picture
Set up materials, film-roll items, finished-bag specs & machines (once)

Production — the stock rules

What moves, what doesn’t

Every shift report posts traceable stock transactions. Three rules protect the numbers — they are why the dashboard can be trusted.

Stage-by-stage stock effects
Stage reportConsumesProducesStock timing
Material Allocation——Reservation only — no stock move, no cost
ExtrusionResin / masterbatch / ink (kg)Film rolls + scrap regrindImmediately on report
PrintingFilm rolls + inkPrinted rolls / rejectsImmediately on report
Cutting / Sealing / PunchingFilm rollsBags (held), rejects, scrapImmediately on report
PackingBags + cartons/tapePacked outputImmediately on report
QC—Sellable finished stockOnly on supervisor approval
Rule 1 — no double deduction

When extrusion turns 60 kg of resin into 2 film rolls, the resin leaves stock and the rolls arrive. When cutting later consumes those rolls, the resin is not deducted again — and the rolls consumed downstream carry zero material cost into the job’s costing. The material is charged once, at the door.

Rule 2 — QC gates finished stock

Cutting produced 5,000 bags but 600 were rejects — only the QC-approved 1,400 that passed inspection land in saleable stock, and only after the supervisor approves the report. Rejected and unapproved output can never inflate what you can sell.

Rule 3 — nothing vanishes
Output typeWhere it goes
Good outputNext stage input / finished stock (after QC approval)
Reusable scrapCredited to the Regrind item — re-extrudes later, keeps its value
Unrecoverable wasteWritten off, costed, visible in job reports
RejectsCounted on the job — excluded from stock entirely
Reversed reportCounter-adjustment rolls every move back; original log preserved as reversed
Traceability: every move posts through the stock-adjustment ledger with the job number and stage in the description — six months later you can still see which shift produced the rolls a customer complained about.

Next → every term, explained once — the glossary.
Reference

Terminology

Every word the Nylon module uses, in plain language.

Item ClassWhat an item is in the factory: Raw Material (resin, ink — consumed), Film Roll (WIP or traded), Finished Good (saleable bags, QC-gated), Consumable (cartons, tape).
SpecThe product definition on Materials & Products: material (LDPE/HDPE/PP), form, bag type, width/length, thickness in microns, colour, print status, design reference, and the measured kg-per-piece / kg-per-roll figures.
Selling UnitA named pack size on a product with an explicit conversion — Bundle ×100, Carton ×20. Orders and sales can be taken in any declared unit; stock always counts in the base unit.
kg per piece / kg per rollYour measured weight figures on the spec. MartPoint uses yours — it never assumes a universal bags-per-kg conversion.
Job OrderThe commercial record in Nylon → Job Orders: customer, product spec, qty + selling unit, agreed price, deposit, due date, design reference, artwork files, dispatch history, status.
ArtworkThe customer’s design file versions attached to an order. Exactly one version can be approved at a time; approved artwork carries over to repeat orders.
Printing GateThe rule that blocks the printing stage until an order’s artwork is approved — enforced on the screen and on the endpoint.
Production JobThe factory instruction raised from an order (or for stock replenishment): product, planned qty, input material, film-roll item, factory, due date, priority, estimated costs, print flag.
Stage PlanThe job’s snapshot timeline: Material Allocation → Extrusion → Printing → Cutting/Sealing/Punching → Packing → QC. Only the stages the product needs are generated.
Shift ReportAn operator’s filed report on a stage: date, shift, machine, operator, qty in, good output, rejects, reusable scrap, unrecoverable waste, notes. Multiple reports per stage track partial production.
QC GateQC-stage output only becomes sellable stock when a supervisor approves the report — the guard that stops unverified output inflating stock.
Regrind / Scrap ItemThe item (set in Factory Settings) that reusable edge-trim and scrap get credited into — it re-enters production later instead of vanishing.
ReversalSupervisor correction on a report: posts a counter-adjustment undoing every stock move, and keeps the original log marked reversed for audit.
Repeat OrderOne-tap clone of an approved order — spec, pricing, design reference and approved artwork included.
DispatchShipping recorded on the order against QC-approved stock. Partial dispatches are normal; each generates invoice/delivery-note paperwork.
Deposit GateFactory Setting: a job cannot be created while the order’s required deposit is unpaid.
Estimated vs ActualThe job’s cost card — your est. material/other costs benchmarked against real consumption per report, yielding margin % and yield %.
