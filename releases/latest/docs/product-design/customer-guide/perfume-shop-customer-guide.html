What a blending perfumery does every day

You are part retailer, part lab. You buy oils, alcohol and bottles as raw materials, compound them into fragrances, let the blend rest, then bottle and sell — in bottles, decants and bespoke orders. MartPoint tracks the whole pipeline and every ml of loss along the way.

Always do

  • Keep raw materials as Not for Sale items — they are consumed by batches, never sold at the till.
  • Set the waste % on every formula ingredient — alcohol evaporates, oils leave residue.
  • Set maceration days on every formula so the tracker can predict the ready date.
  • Log every loss — evaporation, spills, broken bottles, counter testers — in Wastage & Losses.
  • Count the lab before ordering — low material alerts fire before a blend stalls.

Never do

  • Bottle a blend before its maceration days are up — the scent has not settled.
  • Adjust stock by hand without a wastage record — you lose the why and the cost.
  • Mix a new delivery of oil into old stock without a batch code.
  • Sell decants without defining them as selling units — stock will not deduct in ml.
  • Complete a batch without enough raw material — MartPoint will block it; source first.

Before your first blend

  • Set the business type — choose Perfume Shop in Business Profile.
  • Check your units — ml, Litre, Bottle, Decant and PCS are created for you.
  • Add raw materials — oils, alcohol, bottles — flagged Not for Sale, stocked in ml/PCS.
  • Have measuring tools ready — graduated cylinders, a funnel, a scale.

Lab habits that pay

  • Name batches like a perfumer — “Oud Royale EDP — 2L — Week 12”.
  • Weigh the bottle run — actual yield vs planned shows your true loss.
  • Store blends dark and cool — and record where each batch rests.
  • Review the waste report monthly — the costliest stage tells you where to improve.

The perfumery golden rule

Every drop has a cost and a destination: into a bottle, into a decant, or into the wastage ledger. If stock and reality ever disagree, the answer is always in one of the three. That discipline is what separates a scent shop from a perfumery.

Tell MartPoint you run a perfumery

Adaeze runs a perfume shop in Lagos. She blends her own fragrances — buying oils, perfumer’s alcohol and bottles — and also sells designer scents, decants and a bespoke blending service. She starts by setting the business profile.

Adaeze, Owner

“I buy fragrance oil by the litre and alcohol in bulk, blend to my own recipes, let them rest for weeks, then bottle at 30, 50 and 100 ml. Customers also buy 3–10 ml decants, and some order custom blends. I need the app to track raw materials, batches, maceration and what all the spills and evaporation actually cost me.”

What Adaeze does in MartPoint

  1. She opens More → Business Profile.
  2. She picks Perfume Shop as the industry type. MartPoint turns on the full kit: warehouse, batch & expiry tracking, multi-unit selling, formulas (recipes), production batches and the Perfumery Workflow.
  3. She saves the profile. A Perfume Lab section appears under Operations on desktop and under More on mobile — and MartPoint seeds ml, Litre, Bottle and Decant units plus fragrance-family categories automatically.
  4. Labels change too: items become Fragrances, recipes become Formulas, production runs become Blend Batches, and staff become Perfumers.
Why the profile matters: Choosing Perfume Shop gives you the entire blending pipeline — formulas, blend batches, maceration tracking and the wastage ledger — without any extra setup. The storefront also switches to the luxe beauty theme automatically.

Sell bottles, decants and bespoke blends

Three customers, three ways to buy: a 50 ml bottle, a 10 ml decant, and a bespoke blend ordered for a wedding. All three draw down the same stock correctly.

Kunle, Sales Attendant

“Customer one takes a 50 ml bottle of Oud Royale. Customer two wants a 10 ml decant to try it first. The bride’s party wants a custom scent — consultation today, sample next week, delivery in two months.”

How each sale works

  1. Bottle filled at the counter (simplest): tap the bulk “Oud Royale EDP” item and pick the 50ml Bottle selling unit — stock drops 50 ml straight from the bulk pool. Nothing else to set up.
  2. Decant: same product, pick Decant 10ml at its own price — stock drops 10 ml. No separate product needed.
  3. Pre-filled bottle (if you bottling-run): sell the Oud Royale — 50ml Bottle product made by the bottling run — stock drops one piece; the liquid and the empty bottle were already deducted at fill time.
  4. Bespoke: a custom order follows the perfumery workflow — New → Quoted → Deposit Paid → Sampling → Approved → Blending → Macerating → Bottling → Ready → Delivered — with the deposit tracked from day one.
  5. Tester: the bottle opened for the counter is logged in Wastage & Losses under Counter Tester — it is a marketing cost, not a mystery gap in stock.

The numbers to watch

  • Yield variance — a run that yielded 940 ml of a planned 1,000 ml lost 6%: more than the formula’s allowance. Investigate.
  • Waste cost by stage — if bottling out-costs maceration, you have a handling problem, not an evaporation problem.
  • Decant sales share — decants should feed bottle sales; track both.

What hides your profit

  • Unlogged testers — every open bottle is a cost; log it under Counter Tester.
  • “Mystery” stock gaps — they are always an unlogged loss or a forgotten batch.
  • Bottling early — a blend sold before maceration finishes costs you repeat customers.

Day one checklist

  • Business Profile set to Perfume Shop (Perfume Lab appears under Operations).
  • Units seeded: ml, Litre, Bottle, Decant, PCS — categories for oils, solvents, packaging.
  • Raw materials added as Not for Sale, in ml or pieces, with real costs.
  • Only if pre-filling bottles: empty bottles added as Not for Sale packaging items, each with its Bottle Capacity (ml) set.
  • Only if pre-filling bottles: bottled products (e.g. “Oud Royale 50ml”) created with Fill per Bottle + Bottle / Packaging Item linked.
  • First formula written with ingredient waste % and maceration days.
  • First blend batch created and moved to Macerating.
  • Only if pre-filling bottles: first bottling run recorded on the completed batch — confirm bottles and bulk ml dropped and the bottled SKU gained stock.
  • Selling units (Bottle/Decant sizes) added to each finished fragrance — this alone is enough to sell.
  • One test loss logged — confirm the stock adjustment and the ledger row both appear.
