The four ways Amara Botanicals earns

A skincare business rarely sells just one way. Amara Botanicals mixes its own jars, keeps a shelf of trusted resold brands, runs a treatment room, and ships nationwide from its online store — MartPoint’s Skincare & Organic Cosmetics profile supports all four at once.

Line A — Your own formulas
Formulate and jar your own products

You buy shea butter, oils, actives and packaging; the formula says what one batch consumes; a Batch Run deducts the ingredients and credits the finished jars. Full chain: purchase → formula → batch run → best-before → sell.

Line B — Resale shelf
Resell ready-made brands

CeraVe, Nivea, The Ordinary — bought from distributors, sold as goods. Each purchase captures the batch lot and expiry date, so near-expiry stock is flagged before it embarrasses you on a shelf.

Line C — Treatments & customs
Consultations, facials and bespoke formulas

Bookable treatments with a therapist assigned and commission tracked, treatment notes on every client, and a full Custom Formulation workflow — quote, deposit, sample, approve, formulate, cure, deliver.

The whole picture
Set up ranges, sellable products, raw ingredients, packaging & services (once)
Buy ingredients, jars and resold stock through Purchases — batch & expiry captured on receipt
Produce — formula → Batch Run: planned → sourcing → mixing → cooling → filling → ready → completedstock checked
Sell — POS, invoices, wholesale sets, treatment bookings, custom formulations
Ship — online store orders, WhatsApp orders, city-based delivery fees
Watch — expiry alerts, production queue, deposit balances, recipe costing

Measure everything in the unit your supplier and your scale agree on — grams for butters, oils and actives keep small-batch maths clean. The seeded unit tree already knows 1,000 g = 1 KG and 1,000 ml = 1 L, so a purchase in kilograms still feeds a formula that measures in grams.

Packaging — Range: Packaging · also Not for Sale
ItemBase UnitCost
Glass Jar 200ml (amber)Piece₦350
Printed Product LabelPiece₦50

Pump Bottle 30ml · Tube 100ml · Gift BoxPieceper supplier

Jars and labels live in the formula as ingredients too — a batch that fills 40 jars deducts 40 of each. Packaging stock now means something on the dashboard: if jars run out before the butter does, you see it in the shortage list before the batch completes, not on filling day.

Do

Give every ingredient a purchase price per base unit. That price is what the formula uses to cost each jar automatically.

Don’t

Never leave ingredients sellable “just in case” — a litre of coconut oil at the till looks like a product, sells like a mistake, and breaks the formula’s ingredient list.

What Completed actually does
Checks stock first — if any ingredient is short it refuses: “Cannot complete — not enough stock: Raw Shea Butter: need 9,000, have 5,400”. Buy or reduce, never fudge
Deducts scaled ingredients — 60 jars ×1.5 pulls 9,000 g shea, 1,800 g coconut, 60 jars, 60 labels… exact quantities per formula
Credits the finished product — Golden Shea Body Butter 200g +60 pcs, at true recipe cost
Posts one stock adjustment — reference PB-0042 [PROD], every line traceable in the ledger
Records a production run — planned vs actual cost lands on the formula’s run history
Then stamp the batch’s shelf life

On Product List → Golden Shea Body Butter 200g → Unit/Variant row, enter Batch/Lot GB-042, Made On = production date, Best Before = +12 months. That row is what the expiry widgets watch — update it whenever a new batch becomes the shelf stock.

Filling Run: bulk bought or leftover product being repacked (a 5 L pail into 30ml travel bottles) uses the Filling Run batch type — a short pipeline (Planned → Filling → Ready → Completed) because no formulation happens. Sale-deplete: the item’s Production Mode alternative deducts raw grams at the till instead of batching — suited to scoop-at-counter products, not jarred lines you want batch-coded.

Next → every term, explained once — the glossary.
Reference

Terminology

Every word this workflow uses, in plain language.

RangeWhat other MartPoint stores call a Category — Face Care, Body Care, Raw Ingredients, Packaging. Drives catalogue organisation and the online store’s shop-by-category tiles.
ClientThe skincare profile’s word for Customer — holds purchases, bookings, treatment notes, deposits and store credit.
Not for SaleThe flag that makes an item an ingredient or packaging: visible in stock and formulas, invisible at the till. Required on every ingredient; never set on a sellable product.
Base / Stock UnitThe unit an item counts in — grams for shea butter, pieces for jars and finished products. The unit tree converts 1,000 g = 1 kg automatically.
FormulaThe recipe in Operations → Recipe Book: ingredient quantities (Not-for-Sale items only), wastage %, yield, and the Final Product it produces. Written once, costed live.
YieldWhat one formula run makes — 40 piece means forty jars of the Final Product, with the jars themselves counted as ingredients.
Batch RunOne production run on the Production Schedule, carrying a formula (or custom order) and a quantity to produce. Batch types: Formulation (full pipeline) and Filling Run (repack only).
Skincare PipelinePlanned → Sourcing Materials → Mixing → Cooling → Filling → Ready → Completed. Completed is the only status that moves stock — and it first verifies every ingredient is available.
Production ShortageThe “Cannot complete — not enough stock” refusal, listing exact needed-vs-available per ingredient — including jars and labels. Buy or reduce the quantity; never work around it.
[PROD] AdjustmentThe stock-adjustment reference (PB-0042 [PROD]) every completed batch posts — the audit trail that turns a stock change into a traceable batch record.
Batch/Lot · Made On · Best BeforeThe product’s shelf-life stamp. Purchased goods capture it on the purchase row; produced jars get it on the Unit/Variant row after each batch — the values the expiry widgets and Stock Rotation read.
Stock RotationOperations screen pairing each fresh receipt with older lots still in stock — the daily instruction for FEFO: earliest expiry to the front.
Selling UnitA declared pack size on a product — Case of 12, Duo Set — with its own price. Stock always counts in the base unit; customers buy in packs.
Sales Margin % (formula)If above zero, batch completion rewrites the product’s sale price at cost × (1 + margin). Set 0 to keep manual pricing — the guide’s recommendation for established price points.
Sale-depleteItem Production Mode that deducts raw ingredients at the POS sale instead of batching — for scoop-at-counter products, not jarred batch-coded lines.
TreatmentA bookable service (facial, consultation): sells like an item, assigns a therapist, earns commission, and accumulates Treatment Notes on the client.
Custom FormulationA Custom Order on the skincare pipeline: quoted → deposit paid → sampling → approved → formulating → curing → ready → delivered. Template fields (skin type, allergies…) are declared on the item’s Custom Order Settings.
Skincare Set / BundleService Packages = prepaid treatment plans; bundles = multi-product sets sold as one price at the till — component stock still moves correctly.
City-based ShippingStore Settings option listing State → City → Fee rows; the checkout prices delivery by the customer’s selected city, replacing flat shipping methods.
Public CatalogueA read-only storefront of your products without checkout — for brands that want presence before they want online payments.
