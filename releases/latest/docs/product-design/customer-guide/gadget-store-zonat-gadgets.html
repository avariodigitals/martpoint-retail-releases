Power products: record the kit clearly

Use separate item records for the inverter, each battery, panels and accessories unless you actually sell them as one stocked kit. This keeps quantities, costs and replenishment clear. Add model, capacity, voltage and compatible battery/panel details to the product name or description. For an installation sale, record the sold items and agree the site survey, delivery and installer handoff with the customer; don’t treat the item invoice as proof that installation has been completed.

Next → warranty — the claim that tests whether the serial work paid off.
After-sales

Warranty — the claim

Six weeks after purchase a customer walks in: “the UPS is not holding backup power.” This is the moment the serial discipline either pays off or embarrasses you.

The claim, step by step
Look it up — enter the serial number or IMEI (from the device, the box, or the receipt) — staff can find the sale and customer, then check the recorded warranty terms
Decide — inside warranty → repair, replace or send to the distributor per your policy; outside → quote a paid repair, no argument needed
Record it — the claim and its resolution sit on the unit’s history, so a second claim on the same device is visible, not fresh news
Replacement (if it happens) — the swapped unit’s serial returns to the claim record; the new unit’s serial attaches to the customer — one clean line of custody
After-sales checks — serial, warranty and service handoff
Customer saysYou verify
“I bought it here last month”Serial lookup: sold here, that date, that branch — or not in the register (grey-market unit, politely declined)
“It’s still under warranty”Sale date + warranty period = in or out, in one glance — no box receipt archaeology
“You already repaired this twice”The unit’s claim history is attached to its serial — the third claim is a different conversation
Customer
I lost my receipt, but I definitely bought this UPS from you.
Turn it on — we read the serial. If we sold it, the register shows your name and the exact date. If we didn’t… it doesn’t.

Reference

Terminology & setup checklist

Every word this workflow uses, in plain language.

Serial numberThe manufacturer’s unique ID on a device — laptops, UPS units, consoles. One per physical unit; captured on the purchase row and picked at the sale.
serial number or IMEIThe cellular device’s unique ID — smartphones and cellular tablets. Same discipline as serials; read it off the box or the device’s own About screen.
Serialized itemA product flagged for serial number or IMEI tracking. Stock counts units, but each unit carries identity — required for phones with IMEI tracking, and computers/power equipment with Serial Number Tracking enabled.
Bulk itemAn untracked product — cables, cases, chargers. Stock is a number; sales take a quantity.
Selling UnitA declared pack on a bulk product — Pack of 10, Carton of 50 — with its own price. Stock always counts in the base unit (pieces).
Warranty PeriodThe stated cover period for a product. Record the actual supplier/manufacturer terms and explain how the customer makes a claim.
After-sales referenceThe sale, customer, unit identifier and service notes help staff review a claim; confirm coverage against the supplier/manufacturer terms.
Wholesale priceThe reseller rate on each product. The POS wholesale button and account invoices apply it — no hand-typed discounts.
Manager approvalThe guard on discounts above a rep’s limit — the sale waits in Pending Approvals for an owner decision.
Branch / WarehouseWhere a unit physically sits — Showroom Floor sells, Stockroom holds. Transfers move stock between them with a full trail.
Price CatalogueThe clean sell-price list the profile enables — the shareable, printable answer to “how much is it?”
Public CatalogueA read-only storefront — catalogue, prices, stock states, WhatsApp CTA, no checkout. For shops that showcase but don’t sell online.
Store CreditRefund value held on the customer’s record instead of cash-out — applies to their next purchase automatically.
Gift CardA prepaid card sold at the till, redeemable in store or online — balance tracked on the card.
QR orderingA printed scan-to-shop code — walk-ins open your online store on their own phone.
