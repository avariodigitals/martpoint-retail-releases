Before anything

Why print is different from retail

A shop sells things from a shelf. Price is on the item; you scan it and take money. A print shop cannot work that way. Every job is a set of decisions — size, paper, quantity, finishing — and the price comes from those decisions. Nothing sits on a shelf waiting to be scanned.

That is why MartPoint's print workspace is built around a quote, not a till. Once you accept that one idea, everything else makes sense.

Job One piece of work for one client. "500 business cards for Zainab." A job holds everything about that work: the specs, the price, the artwork, the payment.
Line One item inside a job. A job for "shop branding" might have three lines: a banner, 24 polo shirts, and a sign board. Each line has its own size, material and price.
Quotation (Quote) A price you give the client before doing the work. It is a promise: "this is what it costs." Nothing is charged yet, and no stock moves. There is no wholesale or retail tier — the quote simply sets the price.
Invoice The bill. This is what the client owes, and what they pay against. Every invoice comes from an accepted quote — never from nothing.
Material What you print ON — paper, card, vinyl, ink. You buy these, you stock them, and you can never sell them to a customer. They exist to be used up in production.
Customer Material What the CLIENT brings you. "Here is my own fabric, print on it." It is their property, sitting in your shop. You must be able to say exactly how much came in and how much is left.

The case study

Zainab walks into Zeon Creatives on a Monday

She needs 500 business cards and 20 branded polo shirts for a shop opening on Friday. Here is exactly what Zeon does, screen by screen.

1
He writes down what she wants Catalog → Material List, to check what card stock and which shirts are in stock. He does not need to guess — the system knows his stock.
2
He creates the job Print Shop → Print Orders → New. One job, "Zainab — shop opening". Into it he adds two lines: 500 business cards, and 20 polo shirts. Each line gets its own size, material and price.
3
He sends her a quote Quotations → New Quote. The system builds the quote from the job's two lines automatically. Total: ₦45,000 for the cards, ₦76,800 for the shirts — ₦121,800. She gets it by email.
4
Zainab says the shirts are too expensive Zeon revises the quote — 24 shirts at a better rate, total ₦113,800. Because the quote changed, the system marks that she must accept it again. This matters: he cannot be accused of printing at a price she never agreed to.
5
She accepts Quotations → All Quotes → Accept. The system records her acceptance against that exact revision.
6
Artwork comes in and is approved Artwork → upload her logo files. Authorisations → she signs off on the final design. Nothing prints before this.
7
The job moves to the floor Production Floor. The banner goes on the large-format press, the shirts go to DTF. Each stage is marked done as it completes.

The case study

Turning the quote into an invoice

The job is printed. Now Zainab owes money, so Zeon creates the invoice.

Step 8 — Create the invoice

On the job, he clicks Create Print Invoice. The system already knows the client, the lines and the agreed price — because it reads the accepted quote. He types nothing twice.

There is no Wholesale / Retail switch on a print invoice. That switch belongs to a shop, where the owner chooses a trade price for a bulk buyer. In print the price was settled by the quotation — so the invoice repeats it rather than offering a second opinion. Offering a tier here would have let the shop bill at a price the client never agreed to.

Step 9 — Choose how much detail Zainab sees

Two ways to show the same ₦113,800. Zeon picks per job, and can change his mind any time.

One line per job (default)
JobQtyAmount

Simple. Good when the client just wants the total.

Detailed. Good for a corporate client who must account for each item.
The total never changes. Switching between the two only regroups the same lines. You cannot accidentally bill a different amount by changing the layout.

The case study

Where the paper comes from — and whose it is

Printing uses up materials. MartPoint keeps two separate piles, and never mixes them.

Your paper

You buy card stock via Purchases. It is marked not for sale, so it can never appear on a till or a storefront — it exists to be printed on.

When the job runs, Production Floor issues the stock to the job and records what was used. Waste is tracked separately from what was used well. Unused stock goes back.

The client's paper

Suppose Zainab brings her own 300gsm card. That is her property, not your stock, so it is never added to your inventory.

It goes into Machines & Maintenance → Customer Materials. The system records what came in and then where it is at every moment: in your custody, in production, finished, or returned to her.

The question every client asks

"I gave you 500 sheets. Where are they?"

Machines

Knowing what your presses actually did

A press is expensive. You need to know which one earns its keep and which one wastes your paper — and you need to know before it breaks, not after.

Counter Readings

Read the meter on the machine and type the number. Do it regularly. This is the ground truth of how much a press ran — it cannot be argued with.

Maintenance

Log every service: date, engineer, work done, cost. Set a service interval on each machine and the system warns you when one is due.

Reports → Equipment Report

This is where the numbers pay off. For each machine you see what it took in, what came out good, and what was lost.

Working with a client

Everything about one client, in one place

Open a client and you get every job, quote, artwork and collection they have with you. You do not have to remember a job code to answer them.

What each tab answers

Print clients get these tabs. Other business types get their own.

Print Jobs & ServicesEvery purchase and job for this client.
QuotationsEvery quote you have sent them, whether they accepted it, and which job it became. This is where you look when a client says "what did you quote me?"
Artwork & ApprovalsFiles they sent, your review of each one, and whether they have signed the proof off. Shows both answers separately, because they are different gate: a file can be approved by your shop but still awaiting the client.
Collection & DeliveryWhat is finished and waiting, what is still with you, and what is still owed on it before release.
Customer MaterialsTheir own stock that you are holding — what came in, where it is now, what remains.
StatementsTheir running account — what they have paid and what is outstanding.
Why the tabs exist when the job screen shows the same things The job screen answers "what is happening with this job?". These tabs answer "what is happening with this client?". A client asking about themselves should not require you to open four different jobs to answer.

Intelligence Report

Your shop, read back to you

The Intelligence Report sits at the top of your dashboard, above License & Usage. It is not a list of today's numbers — you already have those. It reads your own records and tells you what they mean and what is about to need you.

What it watches

In a print shop, these appear when the records support them:

• Jobs waiting on a deposit, so work cannot start

• Jobs waiting on the client's artwork approval

• Which client holds the largest outstanding balance

• Client-owned material you are holding

• A machine due for service, or past its date

• A machine needing repeat repairs

• Whether job intake is rising or falling

• Which client settles fastest

What it will not do

It will not invent anything. Every line is built from rows that exist in your own records.

If you have no service history for a machine, it stays quiet about that machine. If a client has only paid once or twice, it will not claim to know their habits — two payments is not a pattern. It would rather say nothing than send you chasing something that is not there.

Two honest examples

"3 accepted jobs are waiting on a deposit — work cannot start until it clears." It counts what is genuinely blocked, not what is merely unpaid. An accepted job with no deposit is work you cannot put on the floor, and this tells you how much of it is sitting there.
"HP Indigo 7900 is due for service in 24 days." It reads the service interval on each machine and counts down to the date. You get told while there is still time to book the engineer, rather than after a press has stopped.
Where to find it Top of your dashboard on desktop, and on the MartPoint mobile app home screen. It is the same report in all places — the phone will never tell you a different story from the desktop.

Terminology

Every term, in plain words

JobOne piece of work for one client. Everything hangs off it.
Job codeThe system's name for a job, e.g. PRJ-20261008-001. Use it on the phone with a client.
LineOne item inside a job. A job can have many lines.
ArtworkThe client's design file. Nothing prints until it is approved.
AuthorisationThe client's written approval to print. Your protection.
Quotation / QuoteA price given before the work. Not a bill. No stock moves.
RevisionA new version of a quote after a change. Each revision needs fresh client acceptance.
AcceptanceThe client agreeing to a specific quote revision.
InvoiceThe bill. Always comes from an accepted quote. No price tier to choose — the quote decided it.
Combined / Detailed invoiceTwo ways to show the same total — one line for the job, or one line per item. The amount never differs.
StageA step on the production floor — printing, cutting, binding, finishing.
Production statusPlanned, in progress, on hold, completed, cancelled.
MaterialPaper, card, vinyl, ink. Bought, stocked, never sold.
Not for saleThe flag that marks an item as production-only. It can never appear on a till or storefront.
Customer MaterialClient-owned stock in your shop. Tracked separately from yours, always.
CustodyMaterial physically sitting with you, not yet used.
Issue / ReturnMoving material out to a job, and sending unused material back.
WastageMaterial consumed by setup or error. Recorded, never hidden inside "used".
YieldAccepted output as a share of input. Your press efficiency.
ConsumableInk, toner, plates — used up by the machine itself.
Intelligence ReportThe panel at the top of your dashboard that reads your own records and tells you what needs attention — what will run out, who is slow to pay, which machine is due. It only speaks when your records support what it says.
Ready to collectFinished work that has passed production and is waiting for the client. Shown on the client's Collection & Delivery tab, with any balance still owed on it.
Shop review vs client approvalTwo separate answers about the same artwork file. Your shop checks it; the client signs it off. A file can be approved by you and still waiting on them — production waits for both.
